Slide 05 · Strategy · Module 1 & 3

Fast becomes complex. Complex becomes churn.

Problem hook

RouteLogic built the most powerful operating system in logistics — and in doing so, buried the one workflow that actually keeps freight moving, so fast becomes complex, complex becomes churn, and churn is already sitting inside our biggest account.

Frontline churn is invisible until it's fatal

Daily users route around the product instead of cancelling.

A competitor is capturing the daily workflow

A leaner rival entrenches itself as the tool for dispatch.

The brand is being repositioned against you

"The expensive back-office layer" instead of the operating system.

Value proposition

Velocity gives coordinators one focused, real-time view, live vehicle status, exceptions, and one-tap reroute, so the tool becomes the fastest way to run the shift instead of the slowest.

Data-backed hypothesis

Based on UXR-04 and UXR-10's qualitative account of frontline adoption failure and renewal risk, combined with Snapshot 2's finding that only 18% of coordinators complete the full daily workflow and Snapshot 3's 30-point NPS collapse, I believe that closing the buried-workflow adoption gap for the enterprise ops manager's frontline teams will result in daily core tasks being completed in-app instead of routed around, as measured by coordinator daily workflow completion rising from 18% to at least 40%. I will protect Manager CSAT on Compliance and Reporting (currently 3.9 and 4.5) and will make a go/no-go decision after a 6–8 week pilot.

PRD highlights · Shift Handoff Wizard · Must-haves

M1 Handoff is a step in the existing sequence, not a new module. Opens in one click once Compliance is done.

M2 Auto-built shift summary: open route exceptions, unassigned/pending routes, unresolved compliance flags.

M3 Maximum of 3 screens: Review open items → Add handoff note → Confirm & send.

M4 One free-text handoff note for context the data can't capture.

M5 Log the Handoff step as complete so it counts toward the 6-step sequence metric.

S1 Demoted: incoming coordinator sees the handoff on open. Tracked in week 1.

What the prototype exposed

The PRD contradicts itself on counts: with the no-double-counting rule, the mock data shows 6 open items (2 · 2 · 2), while PRD copy says "7 open items" and "3 route exceptions".

Kept: the no-double-counting rule. Mock data or two lines of PRD copy need to change.

What the PRD makes explicit: the functional requirements, how they are traced and how to check.

Slide 08 · Validation · Module 5

A/B test: Shift Handoff Wizard vs today's handoff

Hypothesis

The wizard lifts coordinator daily workflow completion by at least 5 points (18% → 23%) without Compliance CSAT falling below 3.9.

A · Control

Today's handoff step. "It does everything, which is the problem": frontline can't find the 5% they use.

vs
B · Variant

One-click summary RouteLogic builds itself, plus an optional note. ≤3 screens, 2 clicks; compliance flags read-only.

Primary metric

Coordinator daily workflow completion rate (Open → Fleet View → Route Assignment → Compliance → Handoff → Daily Report)

4.5Reporting

~1,020 coordinator-days per arm (95% confidence, 80% power)

Slide 10 · Story

The fix was subtraction, not features.

Aha moment

Coordinators did not need more power, they needed the live view unburied.

Friction

The hardest part was protecting enterprise depth while making the frontline the default.

Key takeaway

For B2B tools, the workflow at the moment of decision beats feature breadth.

What I'd do next
  • Instrument the reroute flow.
  • A/B test exception-alert timing to cut time-to-action further.
